How it works

Sediment enters at the left edge and moves by water-depth-dependent diffusion, sand less mobile than mud. When the shoreline nears the shelf edge, part of the supply runs out as turbidity currents that bypass the slope and deposit where the gradient eases. Every column compacts as fluid drains through the mud; if it can't drain fast enough, pressure builds. Under the sediment, a salt layer (pink) flows like a very stiff fluid, squeezed from where the load on it is high to where it is low; the sediment columns ride down where salt leaves and up where it gathers. Salt and sediment both balance: see the checks on the right.

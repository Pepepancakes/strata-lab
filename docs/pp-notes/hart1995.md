# Hart, Flemings and Deshpande 1995, Porosity and pressure: compaction disequilibrium in a Gulf Coast Pleistocene basin

Deep-read note (LIT-PP-2a). Four journal pages, four equations, one well pair. Pages are journal pages 45-48.
Claims taken from others are written "Hart et al. (citing X)". Nothing in `src/`, `tests/` or presets was changed.

## 1. Record

- Authors: Bruce S. Hart, Peter B. Flemings, Anuradha Deshpande.
- Year: 1995.
- Title: Porosity and pressure: Role of compaction disequilibrium in the development of geopressures in a Gulf Coast
  Pleistocene basin.
- Journal: Geology, January 1995, v. 23, no. 1, p. 45-48; 5 figures. No DOI printed on the copy.
- Access: full text, John's copy (4 pages read; the text layer loses minus signs in exponents, so the two compaction
  coefficients were confirmed on the page images).

## 2. What it is

A short field study. The authors fit an exponential porosity-effective stress law to shale porosity in the shallow,
hydrostatically pressured section of one well (331 SH #1, Eugene Island block 330, offshore Louisiana), then invert it
for fluid pressure in the nearby Pathfinder well, which crossed three growth-fault splays (A, B, D) into geopressure.
Predicted pressures match measurements down to the JD sand and fall 3-5 MPa short below the D fault; they conclude
that about three-quarters of the overpressure is compaction disequilibrium and the remaining 4 MPa is a late source
(illitisation, aquathermal or gas) that unloads the shale. SI units (m, MPa).

## 3. Equations

Symbols: phi porosity; phi_0 reference porosity; beta compaction coefficient, MPa^-1; sigma effective stress, MPa;
P_l lithostatic (overburden) pressure; P_f fluid pressure; delta t sonic travel time, delta t_ma matrix travel time,
microseconds per m; x a constant.

| No. | Page | Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 46 | phi = phi_0 exp(-beta sigma) | Exponential porosity-effective stress law (Hart et al., citing Rubey & Hubbert 1959 building on Athy 1930). |
| 2 | 46 | sigma = P_l - P_f | Terzaghi effective stress (citing Terzaghi & Peck 1948). |
| 3 | 46 | P_f = P_l - (1/beta) ln(phi_0 / phi) | Eqs. 1 and 2 inverted for fluid pressure from porosity. |
| 4 | 46 | phi = 1 - (delta t_ma / delta t)^(1/x) | Sonic porosity transform (citing Issler 1992), with delta t_ma = 220 microseconds/m and x = 2.19 for low-TOC, non-calcareous shales. |

Hand check of Eq. 3 with the paper's numbers: at the base of the hydrostatic fit (sigma = 12 MPa) Eq. 1 gives phi =
0.40 exp(-0.0368 x 12) = 0.257. At the deepest measured point (2197 m, P_l = 45.2 MPa, P_f = 42.2 MPa, sigma = 3.0 MPa)
Eq. 1 predicts phi = 0.36; the Fig. 5 unloading argument says the shale actually sits at the porosity it reached at
7-8 MPa (about 0.30), which is why Eq. 3 underpredicts there.

## 5. Data the law was fitted to

One well (331 SH #1, the closest with shallow sonic, gamma ray and density logs): gamma ray to pick shales, smoothed
sonic for porosity (Eq. 4), hydrostatic effective stress to 1500 m. Tested on one well (Pathfinder) with wireline logs,
sidewall cores and pressure measurements. No permeability measured; the time-dependence of pressure dissipation is
discussed (Bredehoeft & Hanshaw 1968 lineage) but not modelled.

## 6. Limits the authors state

- Eq. 1 does not describe unloading: compaction is irreversible, so when effective stress falls the porosity follows an
  approximately horizontal unloading curve (citing Karig & Hou 1992) and Eq. 3 underpredicts pressure (p. 47).
- Eq. 3 assumes matrix properties (beta) do not change with time; secondary consolidation or diagenesis would raise
  P_f beyond the model (p. 47).
- Borehole rugosity affects the sonic log and gives the sawtooth in the predicted curve (p. 46).
- Shales are scarce between the B and D faults, so no trend can be read there (p. 46).
- They hope future studies will show whether this beta is observed elsewhere (p. 48).

## 7. Relevance to Strata Lab

- `src/engine/compaction.ts` lines 30-32 use `phi(sigma) = phi0 exp(-beta sigma)`, the **same law as Eq. 1**, with
  the engine's `beta` in Pa^-1 and sigma in Pa. Hart's fitted pair (phi_0 = 0.40, beta = 3.68 x 10^-2 MPa^-1 = 3.68 x
  10^-8 Pa^-1) is a published shale value for a Gulf Coast Pleistocene section. The engine's mud defaults in
  `src/engine/params.ts` lines 99-102 are phi_0 = 0.63 and beta = 5.2 x 10^-8 Pa^-1 (5.2 x 10^-2 MPa^-1), converted
  from Sclater & Christie (1980) depth coefficients, not from Hart. The engine's beta sits between Hart's two curves
  (3.68 and 7.2 x 10^-2 MPa^-1); the engine's phi_0 is well above Hart's 0.40, which is a sonic-derived shale value
  at the top of a well, not a seafloor porosity. Both can now be cited to p. 46 and Fig. 2 as a check, with that
  difference in meaning stated.
- `src/engine/wellPressure.ts`: the engine's pore pressure comes from the compaction solver, not from an inversion of
  porosity; but Eq. 3 is exactly the inverse of the engine's law and is a one-line check that a given (phi, S) pair
  implies the engine's P. Overpressure of 15 MPa at 2.2 km with P_f reaching 93 % of lithostatic is a published
  magnitude for the mud-weight window at shallow depth in a rapidly deposited Gulf Coast section (D22 salt/minibasin
  context, AUD-3 "pore pressure in a Pleistocene minibasin").
- Unloading: the engine's compaction law is also irreversible-free (Eq. 1 only); Hart et al. state the same limit.
  Anything the model says about pressure after a late source (gas, illitisation) is outside both. Worth a "not claimed"
  line in `physics-references.md`.
- Hydrostatic gradient 10.5 MPa/km (1,070 kg/m^3 brine) is a published check for the engine's `rhoWater`.
- Not in Hart: fracture gradient, leak-off, salt, stress ratio, water depth (EI 330 is shelf; subsea depths only).

The one finding that matters most for M4: the engine's exponential porosity-effective stress law is Hart's Eq. 1, and
Hart's fitted Gulf Coast shale pair (phi_0 = 0.40, beta = 3.68 x 10^-2 MPa^-1) is a citable default; the law
underpredicts pressure where unloading has occurred, which the engine does not represent either.

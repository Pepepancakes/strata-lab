## Findings

### Finding 1 (BLOCKING, small fix): a wellhead on land has two different rig floors
Where the ground at the wellhead is above sea level, the depths and the pressures use different rig floors:
- `src/engine/well.ts` `wellStations` (line 159): `tvdssFt = tvd - airGapFt`, so MD 0 (the rig floor) is always the air gap above the SEA, whatever the ground does.
- `src/engine/well.ts` `sampleWell` (lines 327 to 329, 362 to 363): the pressure datum is the ground, `rkbElev = ground + air gap`, and EMW divides by `depth below the ground + air gap`.

So on the Gulf preset at the default wellhead (69.5 km, 0 Ma; the ground is about 117 ft above sea level there, water depth 0) the first station, MD 0 / `tvd_rkb_ft` 0, is 37 ft underground: it is `sediment` with a pore pressure of 16.6 psi and 2.72 ppg hydrostatic EMW at "zero depth". The panel's flag reads "Narrowest window: 1.47 ppg at -80 ft TVDSS (MD 0 ft)". Every MD in that well is 117 ft short of the distance from the real rig floor (TD 30,000 ft TVDSS is reached at MD 30,080, really 30,197; the J wells' kickoff at 5,000 ft TVDSS is MD 5,080, really 5,197), and the CSV's two "from the rig floor" quantities disagree: `pore_pressure_psi = pore_pressure_ppg x 0.051966 x tvd_rkb_ft` misses by up to 98 psi in the Gulf CSV, while it holds to 0.003 psi in the default (offshore) preset (see the "record-independent" lines in the output log). The documentation says the opposite of what happens: `docs/physics-references.md` line 334 and the header of `well.ts` (line 20) say "stations above that rig floor are reported as air"; with the station origin at sea level + air gap no station can ever lie above `rkbElev` (line 348 is dead code when the ground is above the sea, and it is the only way to reach that branch). `tests/wellSample.test.ts` has no case with the ground above the sea.

For an offshore wellhead (the default preset, every deep-water case) both rig floors coincide and nothing here applies; the hand calculation below matches the app at every station.

Minimum fix (recommended): in `sampleWell`, measure the path from the real rig floor: shift every station by the ground's height above the sea where it is positive (`groundFt = max(0, hHead - sl)` in ft; `tvdssFt[i] = tvdFt[i] - airGapFt - groundFt`, same for the J-well kickoff). Then `tvd_rkb_ft` equals the EMW depth, MD 0 is the rig floor, and the first stations are air down to the ground. Report the ground height in the panel ("wellhead on land, ground 117 ft above sea level") and add a `wellSample` test with a column whose surface is above the sea. Alternative if John prefers depths from sea level + air gap even on land: say so in the CSV header and the docs, print `depthRkbFt` as the EMW depth column, and delete the "reported as air" sentence. Either way the docs and the code must say the same thing.

### Finding 2 (NON-BLOCKING): the window in salt is hydrostatic to overburden, 9.9 ppg wide
In the Gulf vertical well (salt 28,560 to 30,000 ft TVDSS in the Node record) the CSV's `window_ppg` is 9.91 to 9.93 ppg through the salt, and the plot shows the same. The panel says "the window starts at the hydrostatic pressure", which is honest, but as a pre-drill prediction it misleads: nobody drills salt with 8.6 ppg mud; the salt creeps into the hole and the mud weight is set near the salt's stress, typically within a ppg or so of the overburden. Minimum fix: in salt do not count a window at all (leave `window_ppg` empty like sea water, exclude salt from "narrowest" and "tight"), and say in the panel that the salt interval needs a mud weight close to the overburden to hold the hole open, labelled "practice, no source" until a source is read.

### Finding 3 (NON-BLOCKING): one stress ratio for all depths
Both methods use a constant share of the effective stress (Eaton nu/(1-nu) = 0.667; Matthews & Kelly Ki = 0.60, both labelled tuned, and `wellPressureParams.ts` says neither paper was read). Traugott (1997, World Oil Deepwater Technology supplement, section "Prediction of Fracture Pressure") lists the deep-water practice: the ratio k = (F-P)/(O-P) rises with depth, his Gulf of Mexico leak-off fit is k = 0.039 (D - W/4 - A)^0.33 (about 0.44 at 1,500 ft below the mudline in 2,000 ft of water, 0.63 at 5,200 ft, near 0.8 at 12,000 ft), and shallow below the mudline the plastic case k = 1 (fracture pressure = overburden) is used. He also warns against a textbook 1 psi/ft overburden in deep water; the app's overburden is integrated from the record's own bulk density, which is right. A Gulf drilling engineer would question the constant 0.667: it is too high in the first few thousand feet below the mudline on the empirical fit and too low at depth. Minimum fix: a sentence in the fgMethod help and in `physics-references.md` that the stress ratio is constant and that practice has it rising with depth (Traugott 1997); a depth-dependent option is a product choice for John.

### Finding 4 (NON-BLOCKING): pore pressure below salt is not exercised and not claimed
The default Gulf well ends inside the salt, so the review never saw a sub-salt station. The engine note at `physics-references.md` line 225 says sediment below salt does not yet count the salt's weight in its compaction, so a sub-salt pore pressure would be the compaction result of a lighter load. Hauser (2020, Mar. Pet. Geol. 111, pp. 105 to 115) finds pressures in salt-sealed Gulf of Mexico sediments tied to the minimum stress at the base of the salt with a near-constant gradient below it, not to local compaction; the Hauser and Couzens-Schultz preprint (not peer reviewed) argues the compaction relation itself should be written in terms of effective stress. None of that is in the model, which is fine for M4 as long as the planned-well documentation says sub-salt pore pressure is not claimed. Minimum fix: add "sub-salt pore pressure: not claimed (salt load and salt-sealed cells not modelled)" to the M4.1 "Not claimed" line.

### Finding 5 (NON-BLOCKING, informational): a browser export cannot be reproduced bit for bit in Node
As documented (D14, M2 review finding 4), the Chrome record differs from Node's: in this session last3 hashed `626d61eb...` in Chrome against `cb22255b...` in Node, gulf `56144748...` against `f2e2fd2c...`, and at 69.5 km the last3 sea floor sits about 60 ft deeper in the Chrome record. The CSV's `scenario_hash` line therefore will not match a hash recomputed in Node. Nothing to fix; the record-independent identities in the browser CSVs (FG = K(S-P)+P, window = FG - pore, hydrostatic datum, salt and sea rows) hold exactly, and the full station comparison was made against the app's code on the Node record.
